Week 9 Engagement Activity

A Week in the Life

Below is one week in the life of a person on supervision: his fixed commitments, and the six supervision conditions he's required to comply with. Try to actually build his week. For each condition, figure out whether it fits into his real schedule or collides with something fixed and unmovable. Where it collides, decide: is that collision the condition doing its job (managing real risk), or is it a structural mismatch that's setting him up to fail?

Meet Marcus

Marcus, 34, was released from CDOC custody eight weeks ago after a three-year sentence and is now on parole supervision in the Denver area. His LSI score places him at moderate risk. He's rebuilding from close to zero: limited savings, no car, and a short list of people willing to house him. Keep his situation, not just his conditions, in mind as you work through this week.

His Fixed Week (These Don't Move)

What The fixed reality
Job Warehouse associate, the only full-time offer he could get. Shift is 10:00 PM–6:00 AM, five nights a week. It's 45 minutes away by bus. Turning it down means no income and a likely employment-condition violation.
Housing The only approved address he could secure on release is a duplex he shares with his older brother, who has a prior felony conviction. No other housing option was available at the time of release.
Transportation No car. The only bus serving his neighborhood runs every 40 minutes, first bus 5:15 AM, last bus 11:00 PM.
Parole office / testing site Open Monday, Wednesday, Friday, 8:00 AM–4:00 PM only. Closed Tuesday, Thursday, and weekends. Random call-ins require reporting within 24 hours.
Money Warehouse pay is biweekly. He has no savings cushion. Each required drug test costs $25, due the same day, at the testing site.
Daughter His 14-year-old daughter lives with her mother across town. His only realistic window to see her is Saturday afternoon, a two-bus trip each way.

Your Recommendation to the Parole Officer

Looking at the whole week you just tried to build: which one or two conditions would you flag for modification first, and what specifically would you change? Your answer should follow directly from the conflicts you identified above, not just restate that "his schedule is hard."
